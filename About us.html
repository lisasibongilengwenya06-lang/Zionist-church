About Us

History

Zionist churches were established in 1904 by missionaries from Chicago. African members later incorporated African spirituality, which was defined as the second baptism of the spirit.

Zionist churches are mostly found in Southern Africa and have a deeply rooted history in the Christian Catholic Apostolic Church. The churches often include words such as Apostolic, Pentecostal, or Faith to represent their biblical identity, for example Gospel Church in Zion.

Zion churches have different branches such as the Zion Christian Church (ZCC) of Limpopo, AmaNazaretha formally known as the Shembe Church or iBandla lamaNazaretha, AmaSiyoni or AmaKrestu of KwaZulu-Natal, Methodist churches, and many others.

The Zion churches have two key characteristics:

  • Faith healing and prophecy – Zionists believe in the power of healing through the Holy Spirit and prophecy. It is deeply intertwined with spiritual practices, where prophecy is seen as divine intervention carrying spiritual healing.

  • Community and identity – Zionist churches believe in providing a sense of community for Black South Africans while preserving African spirituality and incorporating Christianity. It is interconnected with community, shaping spiritual identities through shared values and social interactions.

Mission and Vision

The vision and mission of Zionist churches is to create a spirit-filled community of believers rooted in African spirituality and Christianity, claiming the Kingdom of God through worship and prayer with dignity while giving identity to African people by nurturing communities through healing, worship, prophecy, prayer, and culture.

Zion Christian Church (ZCC)

The Zion Christian Church was founded in 1910 by Engenas Barnabas Lekganyane in Thabakgone, Limpopo. It is the largest African Independent Church in southern Africa, with an estimated membership of over 15 million people.

The ZCC headquarters is located at Moria in Limpopo, which serves as a pilgrimage site where millions of members gather during Easter and September for their annual festivals. Members are identified by their distinctive star badge worn on their clothing.

  • Founded: 1910 by Engenas Lekganyane
  • Headquarters: Moria, Limpopo
  • Symbol: The five-pointed star badge
  • Key practices: Faith healing, prophecy, communal worship

Shembe / AmaNazaretha (Nazareth Baptist Church)

The Nazareth Baptist Church was founded in 1910/1911 by Isaiah Mloyiswa Mdliwamafa Shembe in KwaZulu-Natal. It uniquely blends Christianity with Zulu traditions, culture, and spirituality. Isaiah Shembe is revered by followers as a prophet and healer sent by God.

Members are known as AmaNazaretha and gather at eNanda near Durban for their sacred festivals. Women wear distinctive white and beaded attire during worship, and traditional Zulu dance forms part of their spiritual practice.

  • Founded: 1910/1911 by Isaiah Shembe
  • Headquarters: eNanda, KwaZulu-Natal
  • Key practices: Zulu dance, sacred festivals, faith healing
  • Identity: Blends Zulu tradition with Christian faith

AmaZion (Zion Churches)

AmaZion refers to a broad grouping of Zionist-style churches found predominantly in Gauteng and other urban areas of South Africa. Unlike ZCC or Shembe, AmaZion is not a single organisation but a collection of independent congregations sharing common Zionist roots.

Common AmaZion churches include AmaZion weGalile, Gospel Church in Zion, and Jerusalem Church of Zion. Many congregations have Zimbabwean origins but are firmly rooted in South African communities today.

  • Structure: Independent congregations, not centralised
  • Common areas: Diepsloot, Alexandra, Honeydew
  • Key practices: Faith healing, prophecy, outdoor baptism
  • Identity: Urban African Christian communities

Methodist Church of Southern Africa

The Methodist Church has a long presence in southern Africa, dating back to the early 19th century. Unlike the other denominations on this site, the Methodist Church follows a more structured Western Protestant tradition while being deeply rooted in African community life.

Methodist congregations in the Zionist tradition incorporate elements of African spirituality into their worship. The church has a strong presence across South Africa, Botswana, and Zimbabwe, serving communities in both urban and rural areas.

  • Structure: Organised denomination with regional conferences
  • Presence: South Africa, Botswana, Zimbabwe
  • Key practices: Communal worship, scripture study, community service
  • Identity: African Christianity with Methodist roots
